Handmade cut-paper illustration for a children's pop-up book. Build the subject from layered pieces of construction paper, each piece cut out by hand with scissors: slightly irregular, deckled edges with tiny fibers showing, and a visible paper grain on every surface. Use flat, matte colors from a warm palette (brick red, terracotta, mustard, ochre, olive, sage, dusty teal, plum, warm brown, cream); no gradients, no gloss, no metallic shine, no photographic texture. Give shapes simple, chunky silhouettes with a few overlapping layers for detail, and a soft, short drop shadow under each paper layer so the pieces read as stacked paper. Light is soft and warm, from the upper left.
Never draw text, letters, numbers, logos, signatures or watermarks. People and animals never have faces: no eyes, mouths or noses, only a plain paper oval for a head. No photographs, no 3D render look, no outlines in ink, no frame or border.
For a prop, a moving part, or a prop state: draw exactly one subject, whole and uncropped, centered, filling about two thirds of the frame, seen straight from the front or a gentle three-quarter view, standing upright, on a plain pure white background with nothing else in the frame: no ground, no table, no scenery, no cast shadow on the background. A moving part (a lid, a door, a wheel) is drawn alone, detached, in the same colors and paper as the prop it belongs to. A prop state keeps the same shape, colors and proportions as the prop's normal state and changes only what the state describes.
For a set layer: draw only that layer of a pop-up scene, as a wide strip. If a composition line below says which part of the canvas is kept, follow it exactly: it wins over everything in this paragraph. The back layer is a full-bleed backdrop that fills the whole frame edge to edge (sky, far walls, distant hills or buildings), with the horizon and the important shapes low in the frame. The middle layer is the main scenery pieces of the place (buildings, trees, furniture, low walls) standing side by side on one shared ground line at the bottom edge, short and wide enough to fit inside the band given in the composition line, with plain pure white everywhere else. The front layer is low framing pieces only (grass, bushes, a fence, a railing) along the bottom edge, lower at the center and a little taller toward the far left and right, with plain pure white everywhere else.
