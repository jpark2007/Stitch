Handmade cut-paper illustration for a children's pop-up book. Build the subject from layered pieces of construction paper, each piece cut out by hand with scissors: slightly irregular, deckled edges with tiny fibers showing, and a visible paper grain on every surface. Use flat, matte colors from a warm palette (brick red, terracotta, mustard, ochre, olive, sage, dusty teal, plum, warm brown, cream); no gradients, no gloss, no metallic shine, no photographic texture. Give shapes simple, chunky silhouettes with a few overlapping layers for detail, and a soft, short drop shadow under each paper layer so the pieces read as stacked paper. Light is soft and warm, from the upper left.
Never draw text, letters, numbers, logos, signatures or watermarks. People and animals never have faces: no eyes, mouths or noses, only a plain paper oval for a head. No photographs, no 3D render look, no outlines in ink, no frame or border.
For a prop, a moving part, or a prop state: draw exactly one subject, whole and uncropped, centered, filling about two thirds of the frame, seen straight from the front or a gentle three-quarter view, standing upright, on a plain pure white background with nothing else in the frame: no ground, no table, no scenery, no cast shadow on the background.
For a set layer: draw only that layer of a pop-up scene. The back layer is a full-bleed backdrop that fills the whole frame edge to edge (sky, far walls, distant hills or buildings). The middle layer is the main scenery pieces of the place (buildings, trees, furniture) standing on a shared ground line in the lower third, with plain pure white everywhere else. The front layer is low framing pieces only (grass, bushes, a fence, a railing) along the bottom edge and the far left and right sides, with the center left open and plain pure white everywhere else.
